# Chapter Twenty-Three: Aftermath in the Factory

Light, first. Fractured, moving, sweeping across the inside of her own eyelids in colours that didn't belong to anything she could name.

Then sound. Not the flat, sustained gunfire from before - something more ragged now, answering itself, two different weapons arguing across the same stretch of gravel. A man's voice shouting something that broke apart before it reached her as words. Glass, somewhere, still finishing what it had started falling an eternity ago.

She surfaced only in pieces, and every piece she surfaced into hurt in some new, specific way that told her, distantly, she was alive to feel it.

*Aniket's hand, gone from her shoulder.*

She didn't know when that had happened. One moment it had been there, gripping hard enough to bruise; the next there was only the chair, and the rope, and a strange new absence where the grip had been. She turned her head - slow, everything slow, her body operating three full seconds behind whatever her mind managed to want from it - and caught, for less than a second, a shape on the ground a few feet away that might have been him. She couldn't hold onto it long enough to be sure of anything - not whether he was moving, not whether he was even the one she thought he was. Her vision slid past it before her mind had finished deciding what it was looking at, and didn't come back to check.

She didn't feel anything about that. Not yet. There wasn't room, and there wasn't, yet, an answer to feel anything about.

*Muzzle flash, twice, from somewhere near the far wall.*

The machine housings she'd registered hours ago as scenery became, briefly, cover - shapes ducking behind them, shapes falling short of them. She understood, in the fragmented, unreliable way she understood everything now, that these were Revanth's men on one side and someone else's men on the other, and that neither side seemed to particularly care whether she survived whatever happened between them.

*Revanth's voice, once, sharp with something that might have been fear rather than command.*

She lost the next stretch of it entirely. Time didn't move through her in a line anymore; it arrived in flashes, separated by gaps she couldn't account for - a body falling near the conveyor belt, gone before she'd registered whose it was. The smell of cordite thick enough to taste. Her own pulse, absurdly loud in her ears, the only steady thing left in a room that had stopped being steady about anything.

*A different sound now - something dragging, heavy, unhurried in a way nothing else in the room had been.*

She couldn't place it. Her mind, what was left of it working, offered no story to attach the sound to, and some self-protective instinct decided, without consulting her, that this particular fragment wasn't one she needed to keep. It slid away from her the way most of the last few minutes had, leaving only the fact that it had happened, not the shape of it.

*Silence.*

It arrived the way the gunfire had - all at once, total, wrong in its completeness. She lay slumped against the rope for a long moment without trusting it, waiting for the next burst that didn't come, and when it still didn't come she understood, somewhere below conscious thought, that whatever had been happening had finished happening, one way or another, without her.

She tried to lift her head properly and managed it only partway. The factory floor, what she could see of it through vision still swimming at the edges, held nothing that moved. Shapes on the ground, several of them, none of them offering to explain themselves. The tinted-glass car's door still hanging open where the stranger had pulled it, his own body somewhere she couldn't locate from this angle and didn't have the strength left to look for.

*Papa,* she thought, distantly, not sure anymore whose voice the word belonged to.

Footsteps, eventually - careful ones, unhurried, the first sound in a long while that didn't frighten her, though she couldn't have said why not. She turned her head toward them and caught, for a fraction of a second before the dark closed back over her, a shape crouching down in front of the chair, hands working at the rope with a gentleness that felt, after everything the night had been, almost unbearable.

She didn't hear whatever he said to her. She didn't feel herself being lifted, or carried, or laid down somewhere that wasn't a hard-backed chair anymore. The last clear thought she managed, before the factory and the bodies and the ringing silence all folded away into nothing, was small and entirely unrelated to anything that had just happened to her - a memory of her mother's hands stilling on a tawa, months ago now, on a night that felt, from here, like it belonged to someone else's life entirely.

Then there was nothing at all, for a long time.
