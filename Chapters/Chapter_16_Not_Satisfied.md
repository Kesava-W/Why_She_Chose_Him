# Chapter Sixteen: Not Satisfied

He told her the next evening, sitting across from her at the kitchen table rather than beside her, as though the distance itself were part of how seriously he wanted her to take it. Aanchal funded the men who'd built the bomb. Akhilesh and Zoya had carried the money that paid for it. A friend of his - a police officer, he said, nothing more specific than that - had confirmed it after months of quiet digging, and once Satya had seen the proof for himself, he hadn't been able to sit on it and do nothing while she kept walking in and out of that office every day.

She listened without interrupting, her hands wrapped around a cup of tea she didn't drink, and when he finished she sat with it a long moment before she spoke.

"That's it? That's the whole thing?"

"That's the whole thing."

It wasn't a lie, exactly, and some instinct she couldn't have named told her that much - he wasn't performing this the way a man performs a story he's making up as he goes. But it was too smooth in the wrong places, too finished, the edges sanded down in a way real explanations rarely came. A friend with a badge. Months of digging. No name attached to any of it, no detail she could have pushed on and found something underneath.

"Who's this friend?"

"It doesn't matter who."

"It matters to me."

"It matters that he was right," Satya said, an edge coming into his voice that she recognised now as the sound of a door being quietly closed. "Manaswini, I'm giving you more than I have to. I could have kept all of this from you entirely."

"You could have. And instead you're giving me exactly enough to make me stop asking, and not one sentence more." She set the cup down, her hand not quite steady. "I know when someone's managing me, Satya. I spent four years being managed by my own parents. I recognise the shape of it now."

He didn't answer that directly, which she took, correctly, as its own kind of answer.

"There's something else," he said instead, after a silence long enough that she'd almost given up waiting for it. "The three men who came for you. I know why now."

She went very still.

"Aanchal thinks you're connected to Akhilesh's death. Someone there believes you fed information outward - to me, and through me, to whoever they assume I answer to. They don't know I acted alone. They think you're the leak, and they sent those men to bring you in and find out how much you knew."

"That's insane. I didn't know anything. I still barely know anything, because you won't tell me."

"I know it's insane. It's also what they believe, and belief is enough to get you killed in this, whether or not it's true."

She stood up, needing to move, the kitchen suddenly too small to hold everything sitting in it. "Then I have to go and tell them that. Explain that I had nothing to do with any of it, that this is all some horrible misunderstanding-"

"You are not going anywhere near Aanchal."

"I am going tomorrow." She said it with a steadiness that surprised her, given how little of the actual conversation had left her steady at all. "I can't live like this, Satya - hiding, not going to work, waiting for you to decide how much truth I'm allowed on a given evening. I'd rather face them directly and end it than keep living inside whatever this has become."

"They will hurt you."

"You keep telling me what they'll do and never telling me why they'd believe what they believe in the first place, or who actually decided any of this, or what you and this friend of yours actually did to Akhilesh and Zoya beyond the version you've given me twice now, word for word, like something rehearsed." Her voice cracked, finally, all the fear underneath the anger finding its way out at once. "I don't know if I can keep being with someone I don't trust to tell me the truth. I've been thinking about that more than I've told you."

Satya went very quiet at that, the kind of quiet that in another man might have meant nothing and in him, she'd learned by now, meant everything.

"Please don't go tomorrow," he said finally, and it wasn't a command this time. It was close, for him, to begging.

"I'm going," she said. "Whether you want me to or not."

Neither of them said anything else for the rest of that night.
