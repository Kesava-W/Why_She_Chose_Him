# Chapter Eighteen: Taken

She heard the lock turn twenty minutes after Satya had gone, and for one clean, furious second she thought it was him coming back to apologise.

"You don't get to lock me in and then just walk back in like nothing-" She was already at the door, already reaching for it, when it swung open on two men she didn't recognise, and the sentence died somewhere in her throat.

She didn't scream. She would try, afterward, in the scattered pieces of memory that survived what came next, to work out why she hadn't - whether it was shock, or the sheer speed of it, or some older instinct that understood, faster than her mind could, that screaming would only cost her the half-second she might otherwise have had. A hand closed over her mouth before the breath for it had even properly gathered. She twisted, got an elbow into someone's ribs hard enough to hear the grunt it earned her, and then a second pair of hands had her other arm, and the flat's small hallway, so familiar an hour ago, became a space with no useful shape left in it at all.

The needle went into her arm almost gently, which was somehow the worst part - not violent, not cruel, just efficient, a small practised motion from someone who had clearly done this exact thing before and expected it to work exactly this quickly. It did. The hallway tilted. The man's face above her blurred at the edges first, the way a photograph blurs when the focus slips, and she found herself thinking, with a clarity that would only make sense to her much later, that Satya's kitchen tap was still dripping somewhere behind all of this, the same small sound it always made, entirely unbothered by anything happening around it.

*Satya.* She tried to say his name and wasn't sure, afterward, whether any sound had actually left her.

The last thing she held onto, before the flat and the two men and the dripping tap all folded into something with no more edges left to it, was the ordinary weight of her own front door key, still in her hand, still cutting faintly into her palm the way it always did when she gripped it too hard.

Then nothing.

Satya's own hour ended in the doorway he'd locked behind him that morning, standing open now, the certainty of it arriving before he'd finished crossing the threshold. He called her name twice into a flat that didn't answer, and then he was calling Vishesh instead, his hands shaking badly enough that he missed the number once before getting it right.

"She's gone." His voice came out wrong, higher and faster than he recognised. "The door's unlocked. Not forced - unlocked, someone's key or someone's skill, I don't know which, but they got in and she's not here, Vishesh, she's not here."

"Slow down. When did you last-"

"An hour ago. I locked her in and I left for an hour and now she's-" He couldn't finish it. He stood in the middle of a flat that still smelled faintly of her shampoo, her toothbrush still exactly where it had migrated to months ago, and understood, with a horror that arrived all at once rather than gradually, that whoever had taken her had known precisely when the flat would be empty of him and full of nothing but her.

"They knew," he said, quieter now, the shape of it settling over him. "They knew I'd be out. They knew when. Someone's been watching this building."

"I'm coming," Vishesh said, already moving, already sounding like a man doing the arithmetic on every resource he could quietly call on without officially calling on anything. "Don't touch anything else. Don't call the police yet - not until we know who has her and what they want, because the wrong call now could get her killed faster than the right one saves her."

Satya stood alone in the doorway a moment after the line went dead, staring at a flat that had, an hour ago, held everything he'd built his last several months around, and felt something in himself go as still and cold as it had the night he'd first decided a needle was a reasonable answer to a threat he loved someone enough to remove.

He had been wrong before, choosing for her instead of with her. He understood, standing there, that he was about to do it again - and that this time, for the first time, he had absolutely no idea if it would be enough.
