# Chapter Twenty-Two: Tinted Glass

The car arrived a little before midnight, headlights sweeping once across the factory's broken windows before cutting out entirely, tyres crunching slow over loose gravel toward the open bay door. Black, windows tinted dark enough that the interior gave up nothing at all, not a shape, not a shadow, nothing for her to hold onto except the fact of it arriving exactly when Revanth had said it would.

"No," she said, the word arriving before any thought organised enough to attach to it. "No, please, whatever you're about to do-"

Revanth didn't look at her. He'd moved to stand a few feet in front of the chair, something long and dark already in his hands, and the casualness with which he held it - low, unhurried, a man handling a familiar tool rather than a weapon - told her everything about how many times he'd done something like this before.

"Please." She was screaming it now, the rope biting into wrists gone raw from hours of small useless struggle. "Whoever's in that car, please, don't-"

The car had barely rolled to a stop when he opened fire.

She would remember, afterward, less of the sound than of everything the sound displaced - the birds she hadn't known were roosting somewhere in the factory's broken rafters scattering all at once into the dark, Aniket's hand closing hard over her shoulder from behind, holding her in the chair though she'd stopped having the strength to fight it. The gunfire itself she experienced almost as a texture rather than a sequence, sustained and flat and endless, the windscreen giving way somewhere in the middle of it, glass scattering bright across the gravel like something almost beautiful if she'd had any capacity left to see it that way.

She couldn't see who was in the car. She told herself that, over and over, in the handful of seconds it took - couldn't see, tinted glass, no way to know, no way to know - and understood, distantly, that she was lying to herself on purpose, building a wall between what was happening and what it meant, because the alternative was believing she was watching her own parents die and she did not have anywhere in her to survive believing that.

The gunfire stopped as suddenly as it had started.

The silence that followed had a ringing quality to it, thick and wrong, and into that silence she heard herself making a sound she didn't recognise as her own voice - not words, just something raw and continuous, until Aniket's hand tightened on her shoulder hard enough to hurt and some animal instinct made her stop.

It was another full minute, maybe two, before the second car arrived.

This one came faster, less careful of the gravel, headlights swinging wide as it braked hard beside the first. A man got out before it had fully stopped moving - well-dressed, older than Satya but younger than she'd somehow expected, moving with the particular urgency of someone arriving to witness something he'd been looking forward to. He crossed to the bullet-riddled car himself, waved off whoever tried to stop him, and pulled the driver's door open with his own hands.

She heard him before she understood what she was hearing - a sound that started as a question and broke, halfway through, into something with no words left in it at all. He staggered back from the open door, one hand braced against the ruined bodywork to keep himself upright, and the name he was shouting into the factory's ringing dark wasn't hers, wasn't her mother's, wasn't anything she recognised.

"Papa. *Papa.*" Over and over, the same two syllables, a grown man's voice cracking into something much younger. "Who did this - who let this happen to them-"

Manaswini didn't understand. She strained against the ropes, trying to see past Aniket's shoulder to whatever the man was seeing, some desperate need to know rising even through everything else, and caught only a glimpse - grey hair, an outstretched hand, nothing she could place as belonging to anyone she'd ever called family.

Not her parents.

The understanding arrived somewhere beneath conscious thought, a relief so total it emptied out everything else at once - the fear, the ringing in her ears, the rope, the factory itself - and the last thing she registered before the dark took her under completely was Aniket's grip loosening on her shoulder as her whole body went slack against the chair, and somewhere beyond that, already beginning, the first shout of the second gun.
